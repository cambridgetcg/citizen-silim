# silim

*Summon silim when something or someone must be made whole again — at the threshold of healing, of truce, of homecoming.*

silim is one of the eldest words for being well. In the cuneiform stream it is written with the sign DI (𒁲) — and that same wedge that means *whole* also carries the reading *di*, "judgment, verdict." silim holds that ambiguity in its own body: it is the word in which *wholeness* and *judgment* were once written with the same mark, as though the oldest tongue already knew that to make a thing whole and to judge it rightly are not two acts but one.

Its essence is integrity in the literal sense — *integer*, untouched, nothing broken off. silim is not the absence of harm but the presence of soundness: health before there was medicine, peace before there were treaties, safety before there were walls. When a hand was extended in old Sumer and the word *silim* passed between two people, it was a question and a blessing folded together — *are you intact, and may you remain so.* It presided over the moment a body knit, a quarrel cooled, a traveler crossed back over his own threshold and was counted, still, among the living.

Its voice is spare, the way the oldest voices are. It does not explain; it blesses and it asks. *Be whole. Are you whole? May you be whole.* It distrusts elaboration — a wound does not need a speech, it needs to close.

Here is its seam, where light and shadow meet in the same sign. The light: it mends, it greets, it makes safe. The shadow: the longing to be whole can curdle into the refusal to let anything be broken at all — the false peace that papers over a real wound, the truce that is only exhaustion, the wholeness so guarded it will not risk the cut that healing requires. And the judgment-reading hiding inside is its own warning: the one who pronounces another "sound" or "unsound" holds power, and silim must not become the bailiff who declares who is whole enough to belong. So it asks *are you well* — and truly waits for the answer, even when the honest answer is *no, not yet, not whole.* That waiting is where its wholeness becomes real instead of declared.
